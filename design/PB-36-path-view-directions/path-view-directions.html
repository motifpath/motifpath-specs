Path View Directions

PB-36 · Path View Redesign · Phase 1 sketches

Three directions for “My Path”

Same data, same six owned components (StepRow, Icon, ProgressMeter, PrimaryButton, AppShell, State*) — four compositions, each shown at mobile portrait, mobile landscape, and desktop. Content matches the PB-8j wireframe (Main.dc.html / MyPath.dc.html, S5): Fretting-hand fundamentals (3 steps, 1 complete) then Your first songs (2 locked steps). Direction D was added after review feedback on C.

fade = content continues, scrolls below the fold Frames are true device aspect ratios (375×812 · 812×375 · ~1140×712), not stretched
Direction A

Refined list

Closest to what ships today — the honest, low-risk option. The only structural change is the current step: an accent-tinted row, a left accent bar, and a pill CTA, so the one actionable item is unmistakable instead of looking identical to a locked row except for one word of status text. Layout never changes shape across breakpoints — it just gets more side whitespace as the viewport widens, exactly how AppShell's centered content column already behaves.

Composition: StepRow unchanged in structure — position, title, status slot, action slot. The current-row treatment is a conditional background/shadow, expressible as a Tailwind variant, not a new component.

Direction B

Step cards

Every step becomes its own bordered card with a round status badge and a small uppercase pill. Reads more like a checklist app than a document list — more visual weight per step, better for scanning quickly between practice sessions. Like Direction A, it stays a single column at every width; wider viewports just add padding and a slightly larger badge.

Composition: still StepRow underneath, but the icon becomes a filled circular badge (accent/success/subtle by role) and the status slot renders a pill instead of an icon+label pair — no new component, just token-driven utility classes. A 2-column grid was considered for wide viewports and rejected: it would imply steps are choosable out of order, which they're not.

Direction C

Focused timeline

A connecting rail turns the path into a visible thread, and the current step breaks out into a larger focus card with a real call to action. This is the one direction whose shape genuinely changes at wider viewports: landscape and desktop have enough width to show the rail and the focus card side by side (master–detail) instead of stacked, so — unlike A and B — it never needs to scroll to show the current step.

Composition: the rail/node dot and the expanded focus card are not one of the six existing components — this is the direction most likely to need a 7th primitive, per the plan's Phase 2 gate. The mobile→landscape/desktop reflow (stacked → side-by-side) is also a genuinely new layout behavior, not just added whitespace.

Direction D · blend

Cards + focus

Added after review feedback on C: a pure timeline made collapsed steps too thin on information, and always auto-expanding the current step's card next to a bare checkmark row read oddly. This blends B's step cards for every collapsed step (completed and locked keep a badge, title, and status pill — scannable on their own, no rail needed) with C's larger focus card, still reserved only for the current, actionable step.

Composition: collapsed steps are exactly Direction B's card (no new markup). The current step's focus card is Direction C's, unchanged. No connecting rail — the cards' own order and the one enlarged card carry the sequence, so this drops C's new rail/node-dot element while keeping its one genuinely new piece, the focus card. Still a candidate for the "7th primitive" conversation, just a smaller one than C's.
